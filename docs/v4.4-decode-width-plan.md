# NVMAI v4.4 — why every post-4.1 attempt failed, and what to do instead

This document is a failure analysis of every performance experiment run after
the v4.1 release, followed by a plan. It supersedes nothing: v4.1 remains
production. It exists because ~20 independent, correctly-measured experiments
all landed inside ±4%, and that pattern is itself evidence about where the
time actually is.

Sources: [v4.2 expert streaming](v4.2-expert-streaming-engine.md),
[v4.3 predictive prefetch](v4.3-predictive-prefetch-plan.md),
[v4.0 core design](v4-core-design.md),
[CPU/ANE co-execution](cpu-coexecution-plan.md), and the
[V4.2 Experiments](https://github.com/Pummelchen/NVMAI/wiki/V4.2-Experiments)
wiki record.

## 2. Why they all failed: the arithmetic nobody assembled

The v4.2 128-token 4-bit qualification reports the decisive split. At
13.623 tok/s, 128 tokens is 9.396 s of decode:

| Component | Time | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Exposed expert-I/O wait | 1.771 s | **18.8%** |
| Other GPU execution + queue wait | 7.392 s | **78.7%** |
| Unattributed | 0.233 s | 2.5% |

**Every single post-4.1 experiment targeted the 18.8%.** The v4.2 control plane
(event synchronization, submission timing, pool layout, GPU classification), the
entire Metal I/O effort, and all of v4.3 are expert-I/O scheduling work.

Amdahl's bound on that component: eliminating exposed expert I/O *entirely*
gives 9.396 − 1.771 = 7.625 s, i.e. 16.79 tok/s, **+23.2%**. That is the
ceiling for the whole programme, achieved only at perfection. Real
implementations captured a fraction of it and paid for it elsewhere, which is
exactly the ±4% band observed.

### And the component does not behave the way the metric suggests

The event-driven path is the controlled experiment that settles this. It
raised I/O-hidden from 12.85% to 20.73% — a 61% relative improvement in the
metric the work was designed to move — recorded 3,315 avoided host waits and
zero completion-to-fixup submission delay, **and still lost 2–3% end-to-end.**

v4.3 is the second, independent confirmation from the opposite direction: it
removed real demand misses (32.6% recall at 79.7% precision) and lost 3.9% at
4-bit.

The mechanism is the same in both cases and it is structural:

> At batch 1 there is no independent work to hide I/O behind. Layer L+1 cannot
> begin until layer L's MoE output exists. Making the expert bytes arrive
> sooner does not create work to overlap them with — it moves the wait from the
> I/O completion into the next dependent GPU command.

This is why "cut out CPU calls and wait calls" has not paid despite being the
right instinct. v4.2 successfully removed the Swift I/O-completion wake. The
wake was never the stall. The **data dependency** is the stall, and it is not a
scheduling artifact.

### Where the shipped configuration actually sits

Published v4.1 4-bit is 16.45 tok/s = **60.8 ms/token**. The bandwidth ceiling
derived in [cpu-coexecution-plan.md](cpu-coexecution-plan.md) is ~1.8 GB/token
at 4-bit against ~64 GB/s achievable on any compute path = 28 ms/token =
~36 tok/s.

So the shipped default has ~2.17x of nominal headroom, larger than the 1.71x
that document claims, because its 1.71x was computed against a 47.3 ms token in
a different configuration. Of that 2.17x, at most 1.23x is reachable by expert
I/O work — and 78.7% of the token has never been decomposed at all.

### The byte budget shows the same misallocation

Per token at 4-bit, from the same source: attention ~900 MB, routed experts
~540 MB, lm_head ~240 MB, shared expert ~120 MB. Attention is the largest
consumer. Post-4.1 engineering effort went almost entirely to the routed
experts, and within them to the *scheduling* of the ~10% that miss cache.

(The 900 MB attention figure predates the KV growth fix and should be
re-derived in Gate 0 rather than trusted.)

## 3. The two "free" resources, honestly

### The Neural Engine

The measured record is more nuanced than "ANE is unusable", and the distinction
matters.

**Dead for decode, and this is not an API limitation.**

- At batch 1 the ANE sustains 64.3 GB/s of weight streaming against the GPU's
  ~65 GB/s. Both are bandwidth-bound and neither exceeds ~64 GFLOP/s. Swapping
  units changes nothing because it is the same wall.
- NVMAI's MoE is built from 0.5 MiB tensors, the regime where ANE efficiency
  collapses: `expert_down 512→2048` measures **11.6 GB/s** against
  `qkv_proj 2048→9216` at 66.7 GB/s. The GPU beats the ANE 2.8x on routed MoE
  and 1.6x on the shared expert.
- A Core ML invocation costs ~0.16 ms. Alternating per layer is 40 handoffs per
  token = 6.4 ms/token, larger than anything it could save.
- Concurrent ANE load raised GPU-busy **+89.3%** and cost 45.4% throughput.
  Caveat worth recording: that load generator streamed 192 MiB per call at
  ~58 GB/s, so it measured *bandwidth contention*, not an intrinsic ANE
  penalty. It does not need re-running, because the batch-1 tie above already
  closes the question on its own.

**Alive for prefill, and it is the largest measured opportunity in the repo.**

| Shape | width 1 | width 256 | width 1024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| qkv 2048→9216 | 247 GFLOP/s | 14,250 | 13,129 |
| expert_gate 2048→512 | 78 | 15,065 | 16,640 |
| expert_down 512→2048 | 34 | 7,845 | 8,223 |

Against NVMAI's GPU prefill at ~600 GFLOP/s — measured at **97.4% occupancy,
maximum DVFS state, Nominal thermals**, i.e. with nothing throttling, waiting or
idle. The gap is architectural: NVMAI's kernels unpack a nibble and apply a
per-group scale and bias for every weight; the ANE decompresses low-bit weights
in hardware and never pays that ALU cost.

And the switching cost inverts: one Core ML invocation per 4,096-token prefill
chunk is 0.00004 ms/token. The objection that kills the decode hybrid does not
apply to prefill at all.

### The CPU cores

Dead as compute, and the measurement is unambiguous: 8 threads of the real
int4 dequant+GEMV kernel during decode raised GPU-busy **+44.9%** and cost
22.6% throughput. CPU and GPU share one memory controller and one package power
budget. There is no split ratio that wins — the GPU is 2.1x faster than all
eight cores combined at the same work, measured with the GPU otherwise idle.

Even a tight *polling loop* on one core cost 15–17% throughput and raised
GPU-busy 10%. An idle core is not a free resource on this SoC.

The useful CPU work is the opposite of co-execution: **remove the CPU from the
critical path**, where it currently spends ~1.0–1.2 ms/token on cache planning
plus a round trip per layer. That is a latency problem, not a capacity problem,
and adding cores to it makes it worse.

## 4. A new finding: the MTP verify pass runs on the prefill path

This was not previously recorded and it changes the standing conclusion about
speculative decoding.

The accepted model is that MTP cannot win because a verify pass costs what the
*union* of its rows' routed experts costs: width 2 touches 12.68 experts/layer
against 8.00 at width 1, a **1.585x** cost, against 1.574 tokens emitted at
57.4% acceptance. They cancel. Break-even needs p > 0.585.

The strongest measured case contradicts that model. Ornith 8-bit, predictable
53-token function, **92.6% acceptance**, 1.926 tokens emitted per target pass —
and the result was **−2.0%**.

Work the arithmetic backwards. If 1.926 tokens per pass yields 0.98x
throughput, the verify pass cost **1.965x a single token**. The union model
predicts 1.585x, and that 1.585x should apply only to the routed-expert
component — attention, shared expert and lm_head read their weights once and
serve both rows via `useTwoRowProjection`. Predicted cost is well under 1.3x.
Measured is 1.965x. **The gap is 0.4–0.7x and it is unexplained.**

The source explains it. `StreamingMTPDecoder` calls
`RealForwardRunner.verifyGreedyPair`, which calls `executePrefillChunk`
([RealForwardRunner.swift:746](../sources/NVMAI/Runtime/Inference/RealForwardRunner.swift#L746)).
So a width-2 verify runs through the **prefill** machinery, not the decode
scheduler:

- `encodeRoutedMoEPrefill` with expert-major grouping and the bounded routed
  tile scheduler, designed for width 4,096
- a fresh `MTLArgumentBuffer` constructed per tile per layer
  (`makeStreamedArgumentBuffer`)
- a separate `MTLCommandBuffer` committed per tile, with pending-tile drain
  logic
- `PrefillStreamedTileBinding.fetchBindingForTile` instead of the v4.1
  hit/fixup cache plan — so the verify pass does not use the decode expert
  cache scheduling at all
- `snapshotGDNAfterFirstToken: true`, a GDN state snapshot per verify
- the MPP tensor path is gated at `tokenCount >= 32`, so width 2 takes neither
  the tensor path nor the decode path

That is a large, fixed, width-independent overhead being paid to compute two
rows. It is a plausible and testable explanation for the entire 0.4–0.7x gap,
and it is an engineering defect rather than a structural property of sparse
MoE.

**This matters beyond MTP.** Width 2 is also the only mechanism available that
creates independent work at decode time — which is precisely what section 2
identified as missing. A width-2 step amortizes every non-expert weight read
across two rows *and* gives the expert I/O something to hide behind. If the
verify pass can be made to cost what the union model says it should, the same
change fixes both problems.

Correction to the standing position: the stored conclusion was "the lever is
acceptance, not the verify path." The 92.6%-acceptance row refutes it. Acceptance
was achieved, and it still lost. The verify path is the problem.

#### Four findings

**1. The control plane is 2.9–3.0% of the token.** `router_readback_ms` is
**0.010 ms** at 4-bit — 0.017% of the token. v4.2's closing recommendation
("the next useful design must remove the router completion/readback and
buffer-binding dependency") targets something worth under one percent. That
avenue is closed on arithmetic, not on difficulty.

**2. The two quantizations have different bottlenecks.** §2 of this document
generalised the 18.8% expert-I/O figure to both. Measured, exposed expert I/O
is **14.2% at 4-bit** and **30.9% at 8-bit**. At 8-bit it is the single largest
item and only 15.9% of I/O is hidden, so there is real room there — the §2
claim was right for 4-bit and overstated for 8-bit. The Amdahl bound on I/O
work is 1.17x at 4-bit and 1.45x at 8-bit.

**3. `head_logits -> embed` costs 15.45 ms at 4-bit and 15.49 ms at 8-bit.**
Identical in absolute terms across quantizations, which proves it is not
weight-bandwidth work. It is 25.7% of the 4-bit token and 13.0% of the 8-bit
token — at 4-bit, comparable to *all* expert I/O combined — and it is paid once
per token, not once per layer. The cause is in source, not inferred:

- `sampleOnce` ([RawCompletion.swift:394](../sources/NVMAI/Runtime/Generation/RawCompletion.swift#L394))
  builds its own command buffer, commits it, and calls `waitUntilCompleted()` —
  a full synchronous GPU round trip per token, outside every instrumented role,
  which is why the merged-occupancy figure never saw it.
- The generic `sample` kernel selects Top-K by **k full passes over the whole
  vocabulary**, each with an inner dedup loop over already-claimed slots
  (`logit.metal:354–362`), i.e. O(k²·V) with V = 262,144 — and it runs in **one
  threadgroup of 256 threads**, on a GPU with ten cores.
- `SampleTopK64` is a proper three-stage tiled reduction (1024-element tiles,
  top-64 kept per tile, parallel across the device) and it is **gated on
  `config.topK == 64`** ([Sampler.swift:161](../sources/NVMAI/Runtime/Generation/Sampler.swift#L161)).
  `GenerationDefaults.topK` is **20**. The fast path never fires in production.

**4. Caveat on the busy/idle split.** `busy_per_token_ms` is merged occupancy
over *instrumented* command buffers only. The sampler command buffer is not
instrumented, so true GPU-busy is higher than the table's 28.20 / 50.27 ms and
true idle is correspondingly lower. Any ceiling derived from those two rows is
optimistic until the sampler is instrumented — which Track S does first.

#### Correction to this document's own decision rule

The rule above specified an absolute threshold (`GPU-busy ≥ 45 ms`). Applied to
8-bit that is wrong: 8-bit reads roughly twice the bytes per token, so its
GPU-busy is naturally about twice 4-bit's, and the absolute threshold fires
"bandwidth-bound" on a token that is *proportionally more* idle than 4-bit
(57.9% against 53.0%). The rule should have been stated as a share of the
token. Read by share, **both quantizations are dependency-stalled**, 8-bit more
so than 4-bit.

### Track S — RESULT: shipped, +30% at 4-bit

Implemented and qualified 2026-08-22. `SampleTopK64` now serves every k in
`1...64` rather than only `k == 64`, so the production default (Top-K 20)
leaves the generic kernel.

The change is three lines of substance: pass `k` into `sample_topk64_final`,
cut `kept` to it **before** the Top-P scan, and widen the gate in
`Sampler.sample` from `topK == 64` to `1...64` with `temperature > 0`.
`NVMAI_SAMPLER_PATH=tiled|generic` selects the arm and fails closed on
anything else; `tiled` is the default.

Cutting before Top-P rather than after is what makes the two kernels agree:
the generic path only ever extracts `k` slots, so the cumulative mass Top-P
compares against is the mass of the top `k`, not of the top 64. Cutting after
would keep 30 candidates at `k=20` on a distribution whose 0.95 nucleus is
reached at rank 30, where the generic kernel keeps 20.

Interleaved A/B/B/A, one binary, one machine state, fresh server per run, a
discarded warmup per arm, published v4.1 profile:

| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `generic` (pre-change) | 16.892 tok/s | 16.810 / 16.909 / 16.891 / 16.892 | 28.10 ms | 15.46 ms |
| `tiled` (shipped) | **21.971 tok/s** | 21.694 / 22.058 / 22.040 / 21.902 | 29.02 ms | **1.40 ms** |
| | **+30.07%** | | | |

| 8-bit | median | runs | busy/token | sampler gap |

**All sixteen runs emitted the identical completion for their quantization**
(4-bit `sha256:671cb07ae4d147cd`, 8-bit `sha256:618ad1efa751f08e`), across
both arms. The speedup costs nothing in output: it is the same token stream,
produced faster. `busy_per_token` rises slightly (4-bit 28.10 → 29.02 ms,
8-bit 50.23 → 53.26 ms) because the tiled reduction is real GPU work that the
single-threadgroup kernel was previously doing inside an uninstrumented gap;
the token still falls from 59.2 to 45.5 ms at 4-bit and 118.8 to 106.7 ms at
8-bit.

The 8-bit margin is thinner because the sampler is a fixed ~14 ms/token saving
against a token that is roughly twice as long. It clears the gate, but with
less headroom than 4-bit — the `generic` arm's 8.017 outlier is the widest
single deviation in either matrix, so treat +11% as the floor of a range
rather than a precise figure.

Validation: 779 tests pass serially, `tools/lint.sh` clean, release build
warning-free, and `tools/golden-baseline.sh --check 4` byte-identical (greedy
uses the fused head and never reaches this path, which is why it is unchanged
rather than re-baselined). `SampleTopK64Tests` pins token-for-token agreement
with the generic kernel across k ∈ {1, 2, 7, 20, 33, 63, 64}, two temperatures
and three seeds at the production 262,144 vocabulary, plus a narrow-nucleus
case that distinguishes the Top-K/Top-P ordering.

#### Steps 2 and 3 — instrumented, and the track is closed (2026-08-22)

**Step 2, shipped.** `sampleOnce` now passes the runner into the sampler
command buffer so it joins the `NVMAI_KERNEL_STATS` timeline as role `sample`.
This is what makes the busy/idle split honest: the caveat recorded in Gate 0
finding 4 — that `busy_per_token` excluded the sampler and every ceiling
derived from it was optimistic — is resolved. Corrected 4-bit split at the
published profile (2 runs, 21.566 tok/s median, digest unchanged):

| | ms/token | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| token | 46.37 | |
| GPU busy (now including `sample`) | 30.38 | 65.5% |
| `sample` kernel execution | 0.910 | 2.0% |
| `head_logits -> sample` gap | 0.277 | 0.6% |
| `sample -> embed` gap | 0.251 | 0.5% |
| residual sampler total | **1.437** | **3.1%** |
| outside body (stop-check, detok, loop) | 6.54 | 14.1% |

True decode idle at 4-bit is therefore ~16 ms/token, not the ~18 the
uninstrumented split implied.

**Step 3, closed without building.** Its own gate was "remove the per-token
round trip *if step 1 shows it is material*". Measured, the round trip is the
two gaps: **0.527 ms/token, 1.14% of the token.** That is below anything the
qualification protocol on this machine can resolve — the promotion gate is
+10% and single-run spread alone exceeds the effect — and the historical
record already measured the same idea class once: the fused greedy head,
which moved sampling work into the head pass to avoid a transfer, came out
~3% *slower* and was rejected. Encoding sampling onto the head command buffer
would additionally thread the sampler through the `LogitProducer` contract
shared by the CLI, server, decode service and MTP, which is exactly the blast
radius that experiment's write-up warned against for a sub-noise return.

The remaining kernel cost (0.910 ms) is dominated by `logit_softcap_softmax`,
which is still a single-threadgroup full-vocabulary pass. A multi-threadgroup
two-pass softmax would recover most of it — also ~1.5%, also unqualifiable,
also not built. If a future change needs a faster sampler front-end, start
there; the shape of the fix is the same tiling the Top-K path already uses.

**Track S is complete.** Final state: production Top-K 1–64 runs the tiled
reduction (+30.07% / +11.36%, identical output), the sampler is visible to
kernel stats, and the residual 3.1% is documented with the reason it stays.

### Track S — remaining steps (2 and 3 of the original plan)

Gate 0 finding 3. This was not in the plan before the measurement, and it is
now the first thing to build: it is contained, it touches no streaming, no
cache, no format and no kernel numerics that affect the model, and at 4-bit it
clears the +10% gate roughly two and a half times over on its own.

1. **Instrument the sampler command buffer** with `recordKernelGPU(role:)` so
   its GPU time stops hiding inside a gap, and re-read Gate 0. Nothing else in
   this track is trustworthy until the busy/idle split includes it. This also
   settles how much of the 15.45 ms is kernel execution and how much is the
   synchronous round trip.
2. **Generalise `SampleTopK64` to any k ≤ 64.** Its stage 1 already keeps the
   top 64 per tile, so top-20 is a strict subset of what it computes; the work
   is passing `k` into the final stage and cutting there. Then widen the gate
   from `topK == 64` to `topK <= 64 && temperature > 0`, which covers the
   production default. Keep the generic kernel as the fallback for k > 64 and
   for the greedy path (`SampleTopK64` asserts `temperature > 0`).
3. **Remove the per-token synchronous round trip** if step 1 shows it is
   material — encode sampling onto the existing head command buffer instead of
   a private one, so there is one fewer commit/wait per token.

Gates: greedy output stays byte-identical and `tools/golden-baseline.sh
--check 4` passes (greedy uses the fused head, so it must be *unaffected*);
sampled output at a fixed seed must be reproducible run to run, and any change
to the sampled token stream must be justified as a numerics change and
re-baselined deliberately, never to make a mismatch go away; +10% median on
fresh interleaved A/B at both quantizations.

Expected, if the sampler drops to ~3 ms/token: 4-bit 60.04 → 47.6 ms =
**21.0 tok/s (+26%)**; 8-bit 119.39 → 106.9 ms = **9.36 tok/s (+12%)**. Both
clear the gate; neither requires touching the streaming engine.

### Track A — ANE prefill (independent of Gate 0; start now)

The only >2x measured opportunity anywhere in the record, and it does not touch
decode, the streamer, the cache, or tok/s. It attacks the largest user-visible
cost: 53 s of prefill for a 3,752-token prompt, and 45 s at 8-bit for the
published 2,397-token long-prompt row.

Scope deliberately narrow:

1. **Attention block only** — 57% of prefill (29,687 ms of 52,350 ms). Not the
   MoE: that would need all 256 experts resident as a stacked tensor, which
   contradicts the bounded-memory product.
2. **Second artifact** — attention weights only, 1.31 GiB at 4-bit, as a
   palettised `.mlpackage` beside the `.gturbo`. Installer and receipt work.
3. **The hard part is the KV handoff** — ANE-produced K and V must be
   consumable by the GPU decode path across two frameworks and two allocations.
   Prototype this *first*; everything else is downstream of it.

Discount the measured 15–19x to 10x for the missing RoPE, output gate, proper
GQA broadcast and KV write. That takes prefill from 52.4 s to ~25.6 s: **2.0x
end-to-end on prefill.**

Gates: Core ML Instrument confirms ANE placement; `tools/golden-baseline.sh
--check 4` unchanged; prefill output within existing tolerance; whole-machine
memory bounded; prefill wall time improves ≥1.5x on the 2,397-token prompt.

### Track B — RESULT (2026-08-22): completed; MTP stays off

All three steps ran to a measured verdict. The B2 pair schedule shipped as
the default verify (it strictly dominates the old path everywhere measured),
but the B3 promotion gate failed decisively, and the failure is structural in
a way this plan did not predict.

**B1 — attribution.** Instrumented per-pass phase clocks
(`StreamingMTPDecoder.advance`, printed by the server's `mtp-phases` footer
under `NVMAI_RUNNER_STATS`). 8-bit, greedy, cache off, 163 passes:

| phase | ms/pass | × scalar token (135.9 ms) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| verify backbone (40-layer width-2 prefill path) | **305.9** | **2.25x** |
| verify head (2 separate full lm_head reads) | 18.4 | 0.14x |
| draft proposal | 15.1 | 0.11x |
| commit / rollback / argmax / checkpoint | ~6 | 0.04x |
| **pass total** | **~352** | **2.59x** |

The union model predicts ≤1.585x for the expert component only, so the union
accounted for far less than half the overhead — the B1 gate passed and B2
proceeded. Three structural defects, all in the tile-path verify:
the prefill tile scheduler at width 2 planned per-tile (2 tiles/layer, 2
sequential fetch awaits), the shared expert was **waited on synchronously**
before tiles so nothing overlapped the fetch, and the grouped tile kernels
cost ~71 ms/pass GPU at width 2 against ~25 for the decode kernels.

**B2 — the pair schedule.** For the t==2 verify only: one union cache plan
over both rows' experts, one parallel miss fetch overlapped with a
non-blocking shared expert, decode phase-1/phase-2 kernels per row (per-row
acts/y buffers, so whole-buffer hazard tracking cannot falsely serialize the
rows), and a two-row lm_head (`encodeLogitsPair`) that reads the model's
largest weight once instead of twice. `NVMAI_MTP_VERIFY=pair|tile` selects the
arm and fails closed; `pair` is the default; sub-16-slot budgets fall back to
tile automatically.

| verify pass total | tile | pair | Δ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4-bit (× 47–49 ms token) | 151.3 ms (3.10x) | **143.5 ms (3.04x)** | −5% |
| 8-bit (× 136 ms token) | ~352 ms (2.59x) | **340.7 ms (2.50x)** | −3% |

Output identity held in every run: nine interleaved A/Bs across both quants
and both arms, every digest equal to scalar decode's. (Verify-path numeric
reassociation does shift *draft acceptance* — the draft consumes verify hidden
rows — but emitted tokens are always the target's own argmax, so the text is
invariant. Acceptance moved 56.4→53.6% at 8-bit across the head change while
output stayed byte-identical.)

**The B2 gate (≤1.35x) was not met, and floor analysis says it cannot be.**
At 4-bit the remaining pass decomposes to: prefill-path attention 35.6 ms GPU
(2.5x scalar's attention — width-2 through prefill kernels), routed pair
25.5 GPU, fetch ~25–30, head 5.4, waits ~15, draft 12+4.5. Even a
hypothetical decode-style two-row attention (~16 ms) and perfect overlap
floors the pass near **1.6x** — because on a sparse MoE the second row's
experts do not share weight reads (only ~3.3 of 8 overlap), the fetch has
nothing to hide behind at width 2, and Track S made the scalar denominator
30% cheaper. The B2 target was set against the pre-Track-S baseline; Track S
moved the goalposts by exactly its own speedup.

**B3 — measured, failed.** The published-protocol scenario class (predictable
function completion), 4-bit, greedy, interleaved off/on/on/off:

| | median tok/s | acceptance | emitted/pass |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| scalar | **14.206** | | |
| MTP (pair verify) | 7.440 | 83.7% | 1.837 |
| | **−47.6%** | | |

Break-even needs pass cost < emitted/pass. Measured pass ≈ 3.0x, benefit cap
is 2.0 at p=1. **No acceptance rate can rescue width-2 MTP on this machine
post-Track-S.** The old conclusion ("the lever is acceptance") and this
plan's correction ("the verify path is the problem") were each half right:
the verify path was genuinely 2x worse than it needed to be, and fixing it
still cannot close a gap whose floor (~1.6x) exceeds what acceptance can buy
(<2.0x) once the scalar baseline is fast. MTP remains off by default; the
pair schedule stays because it is strictly better wherever MTP is used, and
any future drafter work (wider blocks, trained EAGLE-family drafts) starts
from an honest verify.

### Track B — original plan

Ordered, each step gated on the previous.

**B1. Measure the verify pass.** With `NVMAI_KERNEL_STATS=1`, compare per-role
GPU spans for MTP-on against MTP-off at matched acceptance on the 8-bit
predictable case. Attribute the 1.965x. Specifically: how much is argument-buffer
construction, how much is per-tile command buffers, how much is the GDN
snapshot, how much is the genuine expert union. **Gate: the union must account
for less than half the overhead, or Track B stops here and the standing
structural conclusion is confirmed.**

**B2. Route width-2 verify through the decode scheduler.** Give
`verifyGreedyPair` a two-row decode path that uses `encodeDecodeRoutedMoE` and
the v4.1 hit/fixup cache plan over the union of both rows' experts, instead of
`executePrefillChunk`. Reuse `useTwoRowProjection` for attention, which already
works. No new kernels; this is a scheduling change on an existing path.
**Gate: verify-pass cost ≤ 1.35x a single decode token at matched acceptance.**

**B3. Re-qualify MTP end to end.** Fresh interleaved A/B/B/A, both
quantizations. **Gate: +10% median over a fresh v4.1 control at p ≥ 0.65, with
byte-identical greedy output.**

**B4. Only if B2 lands — reconsider expert I/O.** A width-2 step is the first
configuration in this engine with independent work available. The 18.8%
exposed I/O that could not be hidden at batch 1 becomes hideable. At that point
the v4.2 event path is worth re-measuring, because the reason it lost no longer
holds. Metal I/O is still not worth revisiting (see §6).

### Track C — Fewer bytes (only if Gate 0 says bandwidth-bound)

The ceiling is bytes ÷ bandwidth and both terms are fixed. The record closes
most of this axis: compression is dead (payload at 93% of its entropy limit,
zlib decompresses at 0.53 GB/s against an 11.9 GB/s requirement); weight
reshaping is dead (already at 88% of achievable bandwidth); 6-bit is withdrawn.

What remains untried:

- **3-bit experts.** Modeled at ~30 tok/s in the v4.0 table and never measured.
  It is a user-facing quality change, so it ships as an added option, never as
  a default. Cheap to test: requantise and check whether `busy_per_token` falls
  proportionally. If it does not, the GPU is not purely bandwidth-bound and
  every estimate in that table is optimistic — which is itself worth knowing.
- **Attention-side byte reduction.** Attention is the largest byte consumer and
  has received no attention. Gate 0 must re-derive its real share first.

## 6. Stop doing

- **Metal I/O.** Four independent disqualifiers: it addresses at most 18.8% of
  the token; it measured 15–33% *slower* than pread in every head-to-head; it
  has no validated `F_NOCACHE` equivalent, so it cannot honour the bounded-memory
  guarantee that is the product; and two of its variants produced a GPU hang and
  wrong tokens. The limiting factor is native sparse MTLIO latency, not host
  callbacks or queue depth — that was measured directly. Retire the backend
  switch or leave it frozen as a research artifact; do not spend more on it.
- **Expert cache policy, hot-set pinning, and prefetch as throughput levers.**
  The ceiling for *any* replacement policy is 93.6% (7,808 of 122,560 expert
  activations are first-touch); LRU at 128 slots already achieves 89.7%. The
  residual miss is a compulsory first touch with no history to predict from,
  which is the mechanism behind both the 0.00% previous-token result and v4.3's
  short lead time. Routing-aware redistribution demonstrated the trap directly:
  it *improved* hit rate and *lost* 8.2% throughput.
- **CPU co-execution.** −22.6%, GPU-busy +44.9%. `CPUExpertFFN` and
  `NVMAIKernelsC` stay in the tree because they are correct and cheap to keep;
  nothing should call them from the decode path.
- **ANE anywhere near decode.** Batch-1 tie at 64 GB/s, 2.8x loss on routed
  MoE, 6.4 ms/token of handoff.
- **Single-kernel micro-optimization of the decode data plane.** Five probes,
  range −0.5% to +1.6%. There is no hotspot; the roles are uniform.

## 7. Measurement protocol (binding for every claim above)

Throughput on this machine carries ±15% run-to-run spread, wider than most
effects being tested, and sequential sweeps manufacture monotone wins by warming
the page cache as they go. Two config changes were nearly committed on that
basis and both vanished under retest.

- Interleave the arms A/B/B/A. Never sweep in sequence.
- Discard the first request after each process start.
- 3–4 samples per arm minimum; report the spread, decide on the median.
- Report `busy_per_token_ms` beside tok/s. If it moves between arms, the arms
  ran in different thermal states and the comparison is void.
- Fixed seed, matched prompt, context, KV precision, cache budget, MTP state,
  thinking mode, and prompt-cache mode on both sides.
- Any change to the runtime or model-load path runs
  `tools/golden-baseline.sh --check 4`. It is the only check that exercises
  real inference; the unit tests never load a model.
- Apply the AGENTS.md model-run preconditions before every measured run.
